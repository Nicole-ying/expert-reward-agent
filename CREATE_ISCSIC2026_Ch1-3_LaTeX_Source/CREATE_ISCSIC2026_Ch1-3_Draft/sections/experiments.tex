\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Design}
LunarLander-v3 is the primary benchmark for baseline comparison and mechanism ablation. BipedalWalker-v3 is used only as an additional validation of the complete CREATE workflow under a different action space and control structure; no baseline or ablation comparison is conducted on that task. Every reward program trains a freshly initialized PPO policy. LunarLander uses $10^6$ environment steps per candidate and BipedalWalker uses $5\times10^6$, with four vectorized environments inside each policy-training job. Each condition contains five independent reward-search lineages and at most ten complete reward evaluations.

The policy is optimized with the generated reward, whereas candidate quality is measured only by the untouched environment reward. We define \emph{search fitness} as the mean undiscounted native episodic return over 20 fixed evaluation episodes and \emph{best search fitness} as the largest value retained within a lineage. After reward selection, the returned policy is evaluated over 100 episodes whose seeds are not exposed during search; this is reported as \emph{test fitness}. The task-solving thresholds are 200 for LunarLander and 300 for BipedalWalker.

All LunarLander conditions use the same DeepSeek V4 Pro backbone, environment description, reward interface, code checks, and PPO budget. \emph{Single-shot generation} evaluates only the initial reward. \emph{Independent-10} spends ten reward evaluations on unrelated initial candidates. \emph{Stateless iterative revision} repeatedly revises the current reward from basic current-round feedback, but has no tool-organized evidence, persistent intervention memory, cross-round component comparison, or hierarchical semantic editing. The two ablations remove either evidence enrichment or hierarchical semantic editing while retaining the remaining CREATE loop. Full prompts, model settings, hyperparameters, and per-seed traces are provided in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Reward Search Under a Matched Training Budget}
Table~\ref{tab:lander_main} separates the effects of candidate count, repeated revision, and the complete closed loop. Single-shot generation never solves the task, showing that an initially plausible reward is not reliably learnable. Independent-10 also solves no lineage despite evaluating the same maximum number of reward programs as CREATE. Hence, search breadth alone does not recover the information contained in failed policy training.

Stateless revision raises the mean lineage-wise best fitness to $44.0$, but still solves no seed. In three of five lineages, the initial reward is already the best version, and the final revision is worse than the stored best in all five. The process therefore produces non-monotonic rewrites without accumulating reliable progress. CREATE instead solves all five lineages at rounds $8,10,2,4,$ and $3$, requiring 5.4 complete reward evaluations on average to first cross the threshold. Its selected policies obtain $231.63\pm12.23$ test fitness, close to their search-time performance.

Fig.~\ref{fig:budget_curve} shows how each method uses additional policy-training evaluations. Independent generation improves only when another unrelated sample happens to be better, while stateless revision reaches a low plateau despite repeated rewriting. CREATE progressively raises its retained incumbent and eventually solves every lineage. The key distinction is therefore not repeated LLM invocation alone, but whether a completed policy-training run becomes persistent evidence that constrains the next reward action.

\subsection{Mechanism Ablations}
Removing evidence enrichment leaves the editor with native fitness, episode length, termination statistics, and component episode sums, but removes the tool-using evidence organizer, activation rates, magnitude shares, temporal trends, and structured cross-round summaries. This variant solves two seeds but exhibits very high dispersion, including one lineage that remains negative throughout all ten rounds. Basic component values can therefore support occasional repair, but do not reliably distinguish an inactive, weak, saturated, or behaviorally unreachable reward term.

Removing hierarchical semantic editing retains enriched evidence, memory, and the best archive, but allows unconstrained multi-component rewrites. It reaches moderate fitness in several seeds yet solves none, and one lineage collapses to approximately $-5511$ in two consecutive rounds. The failure is not simply a lack of information: when several unrelated components change together, one subsequent training result cannot identify which intervention should be retained or reverted. CREATE instead selects one principal semantic and applies an L1 parameter repair, L2 component refactor, or exceptional L3 redesign, making each expensive retraining run a more focused intervention test.

Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_scatter} makes the reliability difference explicit. Evidence-poor repair can occasionally succeed but is strongly initialization-dependent. Unrestricted editing has lower dispersion only because all five lineages remain below the threshold. Full CREATE combines a $5/5$ solution rate with a substantially tighter distribution, supporting the need to couple enriched observation with bounded reward action.

\subsection{Representative Reward-Repair Patterns}
The aggregate results do not reveal how component evidence is translated into a concrete edit. Fig.~\ref{fig:repair_patterns} therefore presents three successful CREATE lineages that expose different failure modes. The plots retain the score trajectory and L1/L2/L3 edit labels, while detailed activation-rate and magnitude-share heatmaps are moved to the supplementary material.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\columnwidth]{fig4a_seed0_repair_trace.pdf}\\[-1.2mm]
\includegraphics[width=0.96\columnwidth]{fig4b_seed3_repair_trace.pdf}\\[-1.2mm]
\includegraphics[width=0.96\columnwidth]{fig4c_seed4_repair_trace.pdf}
\caption{Three representative CREATE repair trajectories on LunarLander: (a) collapse and recovery in seed~0, (b) progressive convergence in seed~3, and (c) proxy-misalignment repair in seed~4.}
\label{fig:repair_patterns}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Collapse and recovery.} In seed~0, rounds 1--6 explore coefficient scaling, sparse-to-dense conversion, and mathematical-form changes without solving the task. Round~7 replaces a state-based proximity signal with differential progress shaping and collapses to approximately $-389$. The diagnostic record attributes this failure to a signal that is near zero when the lander is stationary and becomes negative under disturbance, providing no stable landing guidance. The round-8 L2 intervention restores a state-based formulation and adds an altitude factor that suppresses reward exploitation, raising fitness to 224. The best archive preserves this solution even when the next exploratory reward falls to approximately $-612$.

\textbf{Progressive convergence.} Seed~3 improves from approximately $-20$ to 148 after replacing an absolute-distance penalty with potential-difference approach shaping. An overly restrictive adjustment then causes a temporary regression. The subsequent L2 edit reconstructs the contact semantic as a reachable gradient, providing continuous feedback from single-leg contact toward simultaneous stable contact, and the next policy reaches 254. Unlike seed~0, these revisions remain within a consistent signal framework and change one principal semantic at a time.

\textbf{Proxy-misalignment repair.} Seed~4 begins at 140 but regresses after a local adjustment. Component evidence shows that a near-ground contact-transition reward is triggered repeatedly during bouncing---about 75 times per episode---and dominates the shaped objective rather than representing stable landing. The L2 repair replaces this transition proxy with persistent contact-quality gating, under which bounce velocity suppresses the reward and only low-speed contact remains favorable. The next version reaches 206 and solves the task.

Across the three paths, the decisive edits address different defects---an uninformative gradient, an unreachable contact gradient, and a rewardable proxy---but follow the same mechanism. Component activation and scale identify a principal semantic failure, and the editor performs a targeted L2 revision rather than an unrestricted rewrite. The trajectories therefore complement the ablation results by showing how enriched evidence and hierarchical editing interact in individual reward lineages.

\subsection{Additional Validation on Continuous Control}

Four initial rewards are already near the threshold and require local refinement, whereas seed~4 is a more informative difficult initialization. Because no BipedalWalker baselines or ablations were run, we do not claim comparative superiority on this environment or broad task generalization.
