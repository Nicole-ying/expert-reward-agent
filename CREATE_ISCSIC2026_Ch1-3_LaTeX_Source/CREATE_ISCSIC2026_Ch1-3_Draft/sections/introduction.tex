\section{Introduction}

Reinforcement learning (RL) depends critically on reward functions that express task intent while providing informative feedback for policy optimization~\cite{sutton2018rl}. Reward shaping can improve credit assignment, but poorly chosen transformations may alter what the policy is encouraged to learn~\cite{ng1999policy}. Inverse reward design further shows that a specified reward should be treated as imperfect evidence of designer intent rather than as the intent itself~\cite{hadfield2017inverse}. These concerns become practical safety and reliability issues when rewards contain sparse signals, exploitable proxies, or inappropriate scales~\cite{amodei2016concrete}.

Reward engineering is therefore iterative. Each completed training run produces rich diagnostic signals---episode length, termination patterns, component activation, and training dynamics---that can reveal how a reward program shapes the learned policy. Converting these heterogeneous records into a precise reward modification, however, still requires substantial human analysis.

Code-capable large language models (LLMs) offer a route to automation. Language to Rewards maps task descriptions to executable robotic reward programs~\cite{language2rewards}. Text2Reward emphasizes interpretable reward shaping through generated code~\cite{text2reward}. Eureka shows that LLM-written reward functions can be improved through evolutionary search and training feedback~\cite{eureka}. Recent systems add dynamic feedback, human preference feedback, chain-of-thought reward engineering, and diagnostic reward refinement to this design space~\cite{card,revolve,cotreward,wang2026diagnostic}. In parallel, LLMs are increasingly organized as agents that observe, reason, and act through external processes~\cite{react,reflexion,selfrefine}. These developments motivate an agent-oriented view of reward engineering in which policy training supplies observations and reward-code revision constitutes an external action.

This paper explores that view through CREATE, a \emph{Closed-Loop Reward Editing Agent with Training Evidence}. CREATE places a reward-editing agent above an inner RL policy learner. After each policy-training run, a tool-using subagent compresses native-task outcomes, reward-component statistics, and training dynamics into a structured evidence summary. The reward-editing agent diagnoses one principal semantic failure, selects an edit level (L1/L2/L3), and applies a semantically localized repair. Intervention memory retains the evidence--action--outcome chain across rounds, while a separate best archive protects the strongest solution.

Experiments on LunarLander-v3 compare CREATE against budget-matched independent generation and stateless iterative revision, with two mechanism ablations isolating evidence enrichment and hierarchical semantic editing. BipedalWalker-v3 provides a secondary cross-task validation. Under a ten-evaluation budget, the experiments examine whether structured observation, persistent state, and bounded reward actions can convert failed policy training into reliable reward repair.
