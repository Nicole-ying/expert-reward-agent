\subsection{Reward Search Under a Matched Training Budget}
\label{sec:reward_search}

CREATE & 10 & $\mathbf{228.98\pm16.54}$ & $\mathbf{5/5}$ \\

\textbf{Search breadth and iterative rewriting alone do not produce reliable reward repair.} Table~\ref{tab:lander_main} tests whether the diagnostic architecture of CREATE---rather than candidate count or repeated LLM invocation---accounts for performance. All four iterative conditions start from a common initial reward (single-shot fitness $-2.21\pm73.38$, 0/5 solved), ensuring that subsequent differences reflect the repair process rather than initialization. Independent-10 fails across all five lineages despite evaluating ten unrelated reward candidates, directly ruling out the alternative explanation that candidate count alone drives improvement. Ten independent guesses, each made without access to training dynamics, remain guesses: failed policy training contains diagnostic information that a fresh initialization cannot recover.

\textbf{Iterative rewriting without persistent evidence produces non-monotonic progress.} Stateless revision raises the mean lineage-wise best fitness to $44.0$ from the shared initial reward but solves no seed. In three of five lineages, the initial reward is already the best version, and the final revision is worse than the stored best in all five. Each revision operates on the current reward text alone---without memory of which prior edits succeeded or failed, and without structured evidence to distinguish a poorly calibrated component from a fundamentally wrong semantic. The process therefore accumulates rewrites without accumulating progress. CREATE instead solves all five lineages at rounds $8,10,2,4,$ and $3$, requiring 5.4 complete reward evaluations on average to first cross the threshold. Its selected policies obtain $231.63\pm12.23$ test fitness, close to their search-time performance.

Fig.~\ref{fig:budget_curve} shows that independent generation improves only when an unrelated sample happens to be better, while stateless revision plateaus despite repeated rewriting. CREATE progressively raises its incumbent and solves every lineage. The key distinction is whether a completed training run becomes persistent diagnostic evidence rather than a binary outcome. Held-out test fitness (Fig.~\ref{fig:heldout_ablation}) confirms that CREATE attains the highest mean with the lowest dispersion.

\subsection{Mechanism Ablations}
\label{sec:mechanism_ablations}

The previous section establishes that the closed loop works. We now isolate \emph{why} it works by testing two mechanism hypotheses derived from the design choices in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\textbf{Enriched evidence enables diagnosis.} The enriched evidence stream (activation rates, magnitude shares, temporal trends, cross-round summaries) enables the editor to distinguish between mechanistically distinct failure modes. Without this information, the editor operates on coarse aggregate statistics that conflate different root causes---an inactive component could be stuck behind an unreachable gate, too weak to compete, or behaviorally irrelevant, and a scalar episode sum cannot tell them apart.

Removing the tool-using evidence organizer leaves the editor with native fitness, episode length, termination statistics, and component episode sums, but removes activation rates, magnitude shares, temporal trends, and structured cross-round summaries. This variant solves two seeds but exhibits very high dispersion ($134.97\pm148.43$; Table~\ref{tab:lander_main}), including one lineage that remains negative throughout all ten rounds. Basic component values can therefore support occasional repair, but cannot reliably answer the diagnostic questions that matter. The evidence supports this link: \emph{what} training evidence is provided determines \emph{which} diagnoses are possible.

\textbf{Hierarchical editing enables single-trial attribution.} The semantic-localization constraint (Eq.~\ref{eq:semantic_action}) ensures that each expensive retraining run serves as a controlled test of a single intervention hypothesis. Without this constraint, multi-component edits prevent the system from attributing a subsequent performance change to any specific modification---the credit assignment problem moves from RL training to reward design itself.

Removing hierarchical semantic editing retains enriched evidence, memory, and the best archive, but allows unconstrained multi-component rewrites. This variant reaches moderate fitness in several seeds yet solves none ($114.21\pm46.31$), and one lineage collapses to approximately $-5511$ in two consecutive rounds. The failure is not a lack of information: the same enriched evidence is available, but when several unrelated components change together, one subsequent training result cannot identify which intervention should be retained or reverted. Each retraining run ($10^6$ environment steps) becomes an uncontrolled multivariate experiment rather than a focused hypothesis test. The evidence supports this constraint: bounded action is necessary for single-trial attribution.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{fig_heldout_ablation_combined.pdf}
\caption{Per-seed held-out test fitness and mechanism ablation results on LunarLander. Horizontal bars mark group means; the dashed line denotes the task-solving threshold (200).}
\label{fig:heldout_ablation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:heldout_ablation} shows that enriched evidence and bounded editing describe coupled requirements. Evidence-poor repair can occasionally succeed but is strongly initialization-dependent. Unconstrained editing has lower dispersion only because all five lineages remain below the threshold. Full CREATE combines a $5/5$ solution rate with a substantially tighter distribution ($\sigma=16.54$ vs.\ $148.43$ and $46.31$). Neither enriched observation nor bounded action alone suffices; together they enable the diagnostic closed loop.

\subsection{How Evidence Becomes an Edit}
\label{sec:evidence_to_edit}

Aggregate results establish \emph{that} enriched evidence and hierarchical editing matter. We now examine \emph{how} component evidence is translated into edits. Fig.~\ref{fig:repair_patterns} shows two representative CREATE lineages; activation-rate and magnitude-share heatmaps are in the supplementary material.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{fig4_traces.pdf}
\caption{Two representative CREATE repair trajectories on LunarLander. Solid markers show search fitness per round; the dashed line tracks the best-so-far incumbent. L1/L2 labels mark the edit level at each intervention.}
\label{fig:repair_patterns}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Collapse and recovery (seed~0).} Rounds 1--6 explore coefficient scaling, sparse-to-dense conversion, and mathematical-form changes without solving the task. Round~7 replaces a state-based proximity signal with differential progress shaping and collapses to approximately $-389$. The evidence organizer reveals the mechanism: the new signal is near zero when the lander is stationary and becomes negative under disturbance, actively penalizing stability. The intervention memory retains the pre-collapse reward in the best archive. The editor uses this evidence to diagnose that the signal lacks a stable reference frame and applies an L2 intervention in round~8: restore a state-based formulation with an altitude factor that suppresses reward exploitation. Fitness rises to 224. The best archive guards this solution when the next exploratory reward falls to approximately $-612$, demonstrating evidence-guided recovery and memory-gated regression prevention within a single lineage.

\textbf{Progressive convergence (seed~3).} Seed~3 improves from approximately $-20$ to 148 after an L2 refactor replaces an absolute-distance penalty with potential-difference approach shaping. The evidence organizer had reported that the absolute-distance term provided an uninformative gradient---constant penalty regardless of approach direction. An overly restrictive adjustment then causes a temporary regression. The intervention memory enables cross-round comparison: the pre-regression version achieved 148 with a specific contact-semantic formulation, localizing the regression to the most recent change. The subsequent L2 edit reconstructs the contact semantic as a reachable gradient from single-leg contact toward simultaneous stable contact; the next policy reaches 254. These revisions remain within a consistent signal framework, and the hierarchical editing constraint prevents simultaneous modification of the approach shaping and the contact semantic---an entanglement that would have made attribution impossible.

Across both trajectories, component evidence identifies a principal semantic failure; the editor applies a targeted revision; and intervention memory preserves the best solution and enables cross-round comparison.

BipedalWalker-v3 validates CREATE on continuous-action locomotion as a secondary domain. All five lineages cross the threshold within three evaluations (Table~\ref{tab:bipedal}); selected policies obtain $310.82\pm5.03$ test fitness. Mechanism ablations are reserved for LunarLander-v3 (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism_ablations}), where component-level attribution is the central question.

In summary, structured evidence, bounded editing, and intervention memory form a coupled diagnostic architecture: evidence without editing provides diagnosis without attribution; editing without evidence constrains action without guiding it.
